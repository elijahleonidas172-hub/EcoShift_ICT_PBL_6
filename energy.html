Reduce Energy Use

Reduce Everyday Energy Use at Home

Small daily habits can lower electricity waste, reduce carbon emissions, and help build a greener future.

Turn Off Unused Electronics

Lights, fans, TVs, and chargers — if you’re not using them, switch them off. Small habits create big savings.

Unplug Chargers & Devices

Even when unused, chargers still consume “phantom” electricity.

Use LED Bulbs

LED lighting uses less electricity and lasts much longer than traditional bulbs.

Maximize Natural Light

Open curtains during the day instead of turning on lights.

Limit Air Conditioner Use

Set it around 24–26°C or use fans when possible to reduce electricity usage.

Use Energy-Efficient Appliances

Appliances with high energy ratings consume less power and save energy long-term.

Take Shorter Showers

Heating water uses a lot of electricity, especially with electric heaters.

Wash Clothes in Cold Water

Cold water works well for most clothes and uses much less energy.

Air-Dry Clothes

Using sunlight and wind instead of dryers helps save electricity.

Reduce Screen Time

Less phone, TV, or computer use reduces electricity consumption and is healthier for your eyes.

Yangon Carbon Impact

How Daily Habits Can Reduce Carbon Emissions in Myanmar

Energy-saving actions may seem small, but when practiced regularly by households across Yangon, they can reduce electricity demand, fossil fuel use, and carbon dioxide emissions significantly.

Electricity Demand in Yangon Continues to Increase

Yangon is Myanmar’s most urbanized region and uses the highest amount of electricity in the country.

Switching to LEDs Can Greatly Reduce CO₂ Emissions

LED bulbs consume far less electricity than traditional lighting.

Unplugging Devices Reduces “Phantom Energy” Waste

Many devices continue using electricity even when turned off.

Reducing Air Conditioner Usage Saves Energy

Air conditioners are one of the largest electricity consumers in Yangon households.
