Clean Energy Choices for the Future

Solar Power for a Healthier Myanmar

Harness the Sun

Solar energy is one of the fastest-growing clean energy sources in the world. In Myanmar, where sunlight is abundant throughout the year, solar panels can generate sustainable electricity for homes, schools, and communities without harming the environment.

Unlike fossil fuels, solar power does not produce carbon emissions or air pollution, making it a key solution for reducing climate change and improving air quality in cities.

Reliable Energy

Solar energy provides a stable and long-term solution to electricity shortages, especially in rural areas. With battery storage systems, excess energy collected during the day can be used at night or during outages.

This reduces dependence on unstable grid systems and expensive fuel-based generators, helping families save money while gaining energy independence.

Public Health & Environmental Protection

Burning fossil fuels releases harmful gases such as carbon dioxide, sulfur dioxide, and fine particles that contribute to asthma, lung disease, and long-term health problems.

By switching to solar power, cities can significantly reduce air pollution and smog levels. Cleaner air means fewer respiratory diseases, healthier children, and improved quality of life for future generations in Myanmar.

Why Myanmar Needs Solar Energy

Myanmar has high solar potential due to its tropical climate and long daylight hours. Investing in renewable energy can reduce reliance on imported fuel and create local green jobs.

Myanmar’s Energy Mix

Most electricity in Myanmar comes from hydropower and fossil fuels, with very limited renewable contribution.

  • Hydropower: 50–60%
  • Natural Gas: 30–40%
  • Solar & Wind: under 5%

This imbalance creates a huge opportunity for solar expansion in Yangon.

Solar Potential in Yangon

Yangon receives 4.5–6 peak sun hours daily, making it ideal for rooftop solar systems.
